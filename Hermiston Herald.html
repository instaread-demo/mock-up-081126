HERMISTON — The Umatilla County Fair this year is a celebration of local agriculture, the arts, culture and community with the theme: Sew It! Grow It! Show It!

The kick-off parade starts at 6:30 p.m. on Saturday, Aug. 8, near Hermiston High School. The route takes more than 100 entries down Highway 395 and into the downtown blocks around Main Street.

The fair opens at 10 a.m. Aug. 12-15 at the Eastern Oregon Trade and Event Center, 1705 E. Airport Road. Daily admission is $15 for ages 11 and up and free for children 10 and under.

A bustling midway features fun and food for every flavor palate. Adding to the festive atmosphere are shows/contests on the Les Schwab Stage and roving entertainers. Fair coordinator Beth Wheeler said except for stilt walker Heather Pearl, the rest of the acts are new this year.

“We like to bring in something different for people to enjoy,” Wheeler said.

For a reprieve from the heat, check out the open-class and youth displays inside the event center. Also, commercial vendors and community groups will share about their products, services and missions.

The animal barns will bustle with activity, as youths with local 4-H chapters and the National FFA Organization compete. The annual Youth Livestock Auction starts at 10 a.m. on Saturday, Aug. 15. Doug Bennett, a fair board director, said they expect record numbers again this year.

“It’s good for the kids and our community really supports them,” he said.

Main stage music cranks up

On Wednesday, Aug. 12, the Wildhorse Resort & Casino Main Stage is tipping a hat to musical acts with Umatilla County roots. Bleachers and general seating are free with fair admission and VIP is $25.

Tylor & the Train Robbers, a red dirt/outlaw country band from Boise, features brothers Tylor Ketchum, Jason Bushman and Tommy Bushman, who grew up in Helix. Opening the show at 7 p.m. is Brass Fire Band. The regional horn group plays pop, funk, rock, jazz, blues and swing.

“Because both groups have a following in the area, we thought we would give people an opportunity to hear them at the fair,” Bennett said.

For more about the main stage shows, visit the East Oregonian website, read the special section inserted in the July 29 edition or search www.umatillacountyfair.com.

Rodeo gallops into arena

Each evening of the fair, Aug. 12-15, the Farm-City Pro Rodeo starts at 7:45 p.m. in the Farm-City Arena at EOTEC. Arrive early for a round of mutton bustin’ while settling into your seats.

It features all the rodeo favorites, including timed and rough stock events. Since its founding in 1988, Farm-City has continued to grow.

Doubling the purse in 2025, with more added this year, firmly plants Farm-City as a top 15-paying Pro Rodeo Cowboy Association event. The sweet payout attracts top cowboys and cowgirls, who ride into Hermiston hoping to take a slice of the winnings, co-founder Dave Bothum told USCowboy.com.

For a fourth year, Garrett Yerigan and Steve Kenyon with 8 Second Media will call the nightly rodeo action. Also, get ready to welcome Kyle “Hashtag” Lamon. Combining traditional rodeo clowning with modern showmanship, he provides laughter and audience engagement as a fan favorite across the country.

Tickets are $24.50-$29.50. Also, on Wednesday’s Family Day, general admission for children is $9.50. For more information or tickets, visit www.farmcityprorodeo.com or call 541-564-8500.
