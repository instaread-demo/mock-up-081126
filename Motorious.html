The Collector's Guide to Monterey Car Week 2026

Every August, the collector car world turns its attention to a stretch of California coastline where Concours judges, auctioneers, and marque historians converge for the single most consequential week on the calendar. For anyone serious about collecting, Monterey Car Week is equal parts market barometer, historical pilgrimage, and social proving ground. The 2026 edition carries extra weight: it marks the 75th running of the Pebble Beach Concours d'Elegance, and the programming across the peninsula reflects that milestone.

A Diamond Anniversary on the Peninsula

The centerpiece of the week remains the Pebble Beach Concours d'Elegance, held this year on Sunday, August 16, on the 18th fairway of Pebble Beach Golf Links at The Lodge at Pebble Beach. Since 1950, this single Sunday has served as the industry's ultimate report card, where restorations are measured against paint-chip-level scrutiny and provenance research pays real dividends. Turning 75 changes the tenor of the show this year; organizers have built much of the 2026 class structure around revisiting the event's own history rather than simply chasing novelty.

The Week at a Glance

Car Week no longer fits neatly into a single weekend, and collectors who treat it that way arrive too late for the best previews and too early for the marquee sales. The unofficial start comes with the Pebble Beach Motoring Classic, a concours-eligible touring rally that departs in early August and rolls into the peninsula on August 12 this year. The center of gravity shifts decisively that same week: the Gooding Christie's Pebble Beach Auctions run August 12 through 15, RM Sotheby's stages its flagship Monterey sale August 13 through 15, Mecum runs its Monterey auction on the same August 13 to 15 window, and Bonhams holds its single-day Laguna Seca sale on August 13. That Thursday, August 13, is also when the Pebble Beach Tour d'Elegance sends entrants out for a public driving exhibition, when Concours Village opens on the Lodge grounds, and when the Classic Car Forum convenes its panel discussions. RetroAuto, the automobilia hall, opens the same day and runs through the Concours itself on August 16.

Inside the 75th Pebble Beach Concours d'Elegance

Ferrari carries the featured-marque honors this year, a fitting choice given the model's presence at Pebble Beach dating back to 1951. Rather than a broad retrospective, the 2026 judging classes narrow in on Luigi Chinetti and NART competition cars, with Carrozzeria Vignale recognized as the featured coachbuilder, its Ferrari, Lancia, Fiat 8V and Maserati commissions with designer Giovanni Michelotti representing a high point of 1950s Italian coachwork. Lamborghini gets its own moment as well, with a 60th-anniversary Miura display separate from the main judged classes. The new classes introduced for the anniversary are where the real curatorial ambition shows: an Early American Speedsters class gathers pre-World War I machines in the Stutz Bearcat and Mercer Raceabout idiom, a Birth of the Streamliner class traces the aerodynamic design shift of the late 1930s, a Road Racing Greats class revisits the 1950s races once run through Del Monte Forest that eventually gave rise to the Laguna Seca circuit, and a Japanese Motorsports class assembles Le Mans-contesting cars from Toyota, Mazda, Nissan and Honda. Collectors consigning or exhibiting in any of these lanes should expect unusually deep judging benches this year, given the historical stakes organizers have attached to the anniversary.

The Big Four Auctions

The auction floor is where Car Week's true price discovery happens, and 2026 brings back the same four houses that have anchored the week for years, each with a distinct personality. RM Sotheby's Monterey sale, running August 13 through 15, remains the auction most likely to carry the week's outright top lot and continues to draw a deep bench of blue-chip European consignments. Gooding & Company, now operating under the Gooding Christie's banner, holds the official designation as the Concours' own auction partner and runs its sale August 12 through 15 with a program that tends to mirror the Concours' own connoisseurship. Mecum's Monterey sale, also August 13 through 15, is the highest-volume of the four and remains the place to find everything from muscle cars to motorcycles alongside the occasional seven-figure European result. Bonhams condenses its entire program into a single day, August 13, at the Laguna Seca circuit itself, a venue that lends itself naturally to competition history and race-proven provenance. Serious bidders should treat these four sales as one continuous market rather than four separate events; comparable lots at different houses can move estimates in real time.

Beyond the Concours Lawn

The week's texture comes from what happens away from the 18th fairway and the auction tents. The Quail, A Motorsports Gathering typically fills a day at Quail Lodge with a smaller, garden-party-style show that automakers use for low-key debuts. The Rolex Monterey Motorsports Reunion turns WeatherTech Raceway Laguna Seca over to vintage racers for several days of genuine on-track competition, and it remains the best way to see cars that never leave the show field elsewhere actually being driven in anger. Concorso Italiano gathers the peninsula's Italian-car owners for a single sprawling afternoon, while Concours d'Lemons offers the week's necessary comic relief, celebrating the deliberately unglamorous and mechanically eccentric. Porsche's Werks Reunion draws one of the largest single-marque crowds of the week. None of these satellite events carry Pebble Beach's date certainty this far out, so collectors should confirm exact scheduling against each organizer's own calendar as August approaches, though all of them traditionally cluster in the same midweek window that leads into the main Concours weekend.

A Collector's Playbook

Lodging on the Monterey Peninsula books out many months in advance for good reason, and anyone arriving without a reservation by spring should expect to stay in Salinas or Marina and commute in. Ticket strategy matters almost as much as lodging: a Concours-only ticket misses the pre-sale previews that matter most to serious buyers, so plan on multi-day access if bidding is any part of the trip. At the auctions themselves, discipline beats enthusiasm; pull condition reports and third-party inspection records before raising a paddle, treat published estimates as a starting point rather than a ceiling, and remember that buyer's premiums at all four houses now run high enough to meaningfully change the all-in math on a marginal lot. Provenance research pays for itself many times over in a week built entirely around history, so any car with a Chinetti, NART, or notable coachbuilder connection this year deserves extra diligence given how directly it intersects with the Concours' own anniversary programming. On the lawn itself, dress leans country-club rather than car-show, and arriving well before the gates open to the general public remains the only reliable way to see the judged classes before the crowds arrive. Traffic along 17-Mile Drive and Highway 1 backs up predictably every year, so building in an extra hour of buffer for any timed event is not optional.

Why 2026 Matters

Anniversary years tend to concentrate both attention and capital, and the 75th Pebble Beach Concours is positioned to do both. The Ferrari and Vignale focus alone should pull significant material out of long-held collections, and the auction houses know it. For collectors, dealers, and anyone simply trying to understand where the market for historic automobiles is heading, Monterey Car Week 2026 is not an event to watch from the sidelines. It is the week the rest of the year's collecting decisions get made.
