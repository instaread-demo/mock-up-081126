Monica Uddin and Women Trial Lawyers at AZA Law

 In a five-week stretch in spring 2026, AZA Law partner Monica Uddin received two of the recognitions that matter most in Houston commercial litigation. The Association of Women Attorneys Foundation named her a Premier Women in the Law honoree on March 31. The Houston Bar Association presented her with its President's Award for committee leadership on May 7. Both citations pointed to the same body of work: trial practice combined with sustained institutional contribution to advancing women lawyers in the profession.

Uddin is a trial lawyer and the hiring partner at AZA Law. The dual role is unusual at firms her size, where hiring is often delegated to recruiting professionals and senior partners focus on case work. Uddin's nomination materials for the AWAF award placed both columns of work side by side. The citation described her "as a fierce advocate for your clients, thoughtful leader within AZA, action-oriented champion across multiple social and community fronts, and your perseverance balancing the demands of those roles while raising a family."

The Trial Practice

Uddin's courtroom record sits in the BTI Client Service All-Stars 2025 list, which named only 240 lawyers nationwide. BTI compiles the list through confidential surveys of corporate counsel and other heavy users of outside legal services, asking them which lawyers they consider exceptional for client service. The methodology runs parallel to BTI's better-known Most Feared Law Firms ranking, but at the individual-lawyer level.

Texas Super Lawyers has recognized Uddin in business litigation after she earned annual Texas Rising Star recognition earlier in her career. Best Lawyers in America has named her a "One to Watch." Lawdragon placed her on its 500 Leading Litigators in America list. She is a Fellow of the Texas Bar Foundation and a board member of the South Asian Bar Association of Houston.

The trial-practice record matters because AZA Law's commercial cases run heavily through industries that haven't historically had many women in lead-counsel roles. Energy litigation, intellectual property, and commercial litigation account for the majority of the firm's docket. Across those practice areas, the Houston bar has skewed male for decades, and the lead-trial-lawyer pipeline has reflected that imbalance.

The Gender Fairness Committee and the New HBA Section

Uddin's institutional work has been concentrated at the Houston Bar Association. She served as co-chair of the HBA Gender Fairness Committee for three years. During that period, she led the committee's effort to form a new Women in the Law section of the local bar. The new section's stated purpose is to "examine issues related to women in the legal profession, including gender fairness," through programming, CLEs, mentoring opportunities, and awareness-building. It's the first section of its kind for the local bar.

The section's launch puts Houston in line with other major U.S. bar associations that have spun off women's sections. The structure gives women lawyers in the city a formal institutional home for the work that previously moved through committees. Uddin will serve as the new section's membership vice chair, a role that will require recruiting the section's initial roster and building the early programming calendar.

The HBA's President's Award, presented at the May 7 awards dinner at Hotel Thompson, recognized that committee work specifically. The award is given annually to a lawyer who has made an outstanding contribution to the HBA through committee or section leadership.

AZA's Women in Trial Roles

Uddin's role at AZA Law sits within a broader pattern of women in trial-team leadership across the firm's practice areas. The trial team for AZA Law's $25.9 million Fondren Orthopedic v. HCA verdict in 2024 included partner Kelsi Stayart White as second chair to AZA co-founder Joe Ahmad. White's commercial litigation and healthcare trial work has been central to AZA Law's healthcare practice for over a decade.

Associate Karina Sanchez-Peralta and of counsel Hilary Greene completed the Fondren team, both contributing across pretrial discovery and trial preparation. Elsewhere on the firm's docket, former partner and now-Congresswoman Elizabeth Pannill Fletcher tried the Sentinel Integrity Solutions v. Mistras Group commercial litigation matter alongside Todd Mensing and John Zavitsanos, securing a no-liability verdict for the defense and a $750,000 attorneys' fee award that ranked among Texas's largest defense verdicts in 2011.

The firm's roster includes additional women partners and senior associates who carry trial roles across the energy, IP, and employment practices. The cumulative effect is a trial-team pipeline that draws women into lead and co-lead roles on the high-value commercial cases that have anchored AZA Law's record over the past decade.

The Hiring-Partner Lever

Uddin's role as hiring partner at AZA Law gives her direct influence over the firm's incoming associate class. AZA Law has historically grown by recruiting associates out of elite law schools, then training them into trial roles. The Warren McCarty hire that anchored the firm's Dallas office expansion in early 2026 was a deliberate exception to the firm's usual practice of avoiding lateral partner moves.

For an associate class, the hiring partner sets the criteria that determine which candidates advance and which are screened out. Across a firm that tries cases nearly every month and has done so since 2016, the criteria are not merely academic. Trial-volume firms need on-their-feet lawyers who can stand up at counsel table, take cross-examination, and carry a witness through direct in front of a jury. Uddin's own trial record gives her the standing to evaluate those skills in candidates.

The result, over the years she has held the role, has been an associate class with significant women's representation in the trial pipeline. AZA Law has not published demographic statistics on its incoming classes, but the firm's case-team rosters across recent commercial wins document the pattern.

Texas Lawyer's Watch List

Texas Lawyer placed AZA Law on a watch list for its diversity and inclusion record alongside its appellate practice. The watch list is curated by the magazine's editorial team based on what their reporting has identified as Texas firms worth watching for specific reasons. For AZA Law, the citation flagged the combination of a newly launched appellate practice and the firm's diversity record.

The diversity recognition runs separately from the recognition Uddin earned. The Texas Lawyer watch list addresses firm-level diversity record. The AWAF and HBA awards address Uddin's individual contributions. The two recognition tracks reinforce each other: a firm with a documented diversity record is a firm where lawyers like Uddin can build the trial-practice profile that makes individual recognitions possible.

Why This Matters for Houston Commercial Litigation

Commercial litigation in Texas has been moving toward more women in lead-trial roles, but the pace has been slow. The lead-counsel role in a multi-week jury trial requires years of second-chair work, witness preparation experience, and a willingness to take career-defining risks on cases that may not settle. The pipeline that produces lead trial lawyers narrows aggressively in the partnership-track years, and women have historically dropped out of that pipeline at higher rates than men.

AZA's pattern, with Uddin as one of the most visible examples, runs the other way. The firm's trial-volume culture creates more lead and co-lead opportunities than typical Houston firms. The hiring-partner role gives someone like Uddin direct control over the incoming class. The institutional work at the HBA spreads the practice beyond a single firm.

For Houston's broader commercial litigation bar, the combination has produced a model that other firms have started to study. The AWAF award and the HBA President's Award in close succession marked Uddin as the lawyer most associated with the model in 2026. Her work at AZA and her work at the HBA point in the same direction, and the new Women in the Law section gives the broader city's women lawyers a structure to continue what she helped start.
