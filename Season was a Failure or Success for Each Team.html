Gauging if the 2026 NFL Off-Season was a Failure or Success for Each Team

Published: 10 Aug 2026 | 12:08
Matthew Gideon

Our long nightmare is finally over. On Thursday, the 2026-27 NFL schedule began with the first preseason game of the campaign. Before we get into the meat and potatoes of the NFL schedule and Week One of the preseason begins, we need to look back at the last six months and determine if the off-season was a success or failure for every team!

Arizona Cardinals: Failure

I was incredibly vocal about how disappointed I was in the Cards' first-round draft pick. I don't care how talented Jeremiyah Love is; he will be disappointing this year with a weak offensive line in front of him. On top of that, Arizona was so desperate for a quarterback that Jacoby Brissett successfully held out for a reworked contract that now guarantees him $15.5 million. Coincidentally, that signing and the stress of a holdout might have been a waste of time after the strong performance by rookie QB Carson Beck in the Hall of Fame Game. Lastly, I am not convinced that new head coach Mike LaFleur is the right man for the job. It seems Arizona HAD to hire somebody, and they ended up going with Mike LaFleur because he's Matt LaFleur's brother and a descendant of the Sean McVay coaching tree. It was a rough off-season in the desert, and that will translate to a bad record during the regular season.

Atlanta Falcons: Success

This season will be incredibly important for the Dirty Birds' long-term plans. They nailed their head coaching hire by agreeing to a deal with former two-time NFL Coach of the Year Kevin Stefanski. On top of that, the Falcons signed running back Bijan Robinson, wide receiver Drake London, and tight end Kyle Pitts to long-term extensions. Getting those three deals done will boost team morale in the ATL while keeping together the team’s three-headed monster for several years. Despite the successes of the front office, there are still massive question marks at the quarterback position. Michael Penix Jr. appears to be the QB of the future in Atlanta, but he has only managed to play in 14 games since he was drafted two years ago. This upcoming season will be crucial when determining if Penix will be a part of the Falcons for many years to come. Still, Atlanta did everything in its power to make sure that the team is set up for success this season.

Baltimore Ravens: Failure

This is a tricky situation because the Ravens didn't have a bad off-season, but they also didn't seem to have a really good one either. Granted, they made a good head coaching hire by landing Jesse Minter, who was one of the best available defensive coordinators this off-season. At the same time, though, they whiffed on a few key moves. First, they failed to complete the trade for Maxx Crosby. While they might have still signed Trey Hendrickson, he's three years older than Crosby and more injury-prone. Next, the Ravens were unable to re-sign their best offensive lineman, center Tyler Linderbaum, who is now a member of the Las Vegas Raiders. Lastly, they lost their tight end of the future, Isaiah Likely, who left in free agency to join John Harbaugh in New York. Not to mention, I do not like the Zay Flowers signing. He is not worth the same amount of money as Minnesota’s Justin Jefferson. It felt like there were more missteps this off-season than there were positive moments.

Buffalo Bills: Failure

Similar to the Baltimore Ravens, the Bills had an off-season of ups and downs. There were some good things, like re-signing center Connor McGovern and landing wide receiver DJ Moore. However, Moore didn't come cheap. Buffalo had to part ways with a second-round pick in the deal. Meanwhile, they might have found a solution for their pass-rushing issues in Bradley Chubb, but he can only be effective if he stays healthy, which has been a problem for the injury-prone 30-year-old linebacker. The biggest problem I have with Buffalo's off-season was the fact that they hired Joe Brady. He could be a good coach, but the optics of his hiring are not ideal. You mean to tell me that the offensive coordinator from the previous regime is the key to more playoff success? I'm not buying it. I can’t confidently say that the Bills are any closer to getting over the postseason hump and reaching a Super Bowl, let alone winning one.

Carolina Panthers: Success

After experiencing their first modicum of success since former NFL MVP quarterback Cam Newton left the team, the Panthers made sure they’ll go into this season ready to once again compete for an NFC South title and maybe even win a championship. They made a pair of huge moves by signing pass rusher Jaelen Phillips and linebacker Devin Lloyd to long-term deals, which are surefire ways to improve the defensive side of the ball. They also revamped the trenches by signing three free-agent offensive linemen and using their first-round draft pick on tackle Monroe Freeling. They also weren't forced to make a long-term commitment to quarterback Bryce Young, who has still had his fair share of issues at the pro level. This off-season was a success, but if they still have the same questions at quarterback a year from now, then Carolina will have a really difficult decision to make.

Chicago Bears: Failure

Considering how far ahead of schedule the Bears are, they didn't have to do much to appease their fanbase. Being able to offload DJ Moore's contract was a huge win for Chicago. I also like their first-round pick, Dillon Thienemann. Unfortunately, that's where the positives end for the Bears. Chicago lost five key contributors in the secondary this off-season. They also suffered a huge loss when Pro Bowl center Drew Dalman suddenly retired at 27. On top of all that, they didn't add a defensive lineman who can make an immediate difference as a pass rusher. That was a key concern the Bears needed to address after finishing the regular season tied for 24th in sacks. Thankfully, they have a good coach and quarterback, which can cover up a lot of deficiencies. Still, this was not a great showing for the perennial off-season champs.

Cincinnati Bengals: Success

Just like the Bills and the Ravens, the Bengals had an even divide between good and bad moments this off-season. Acquiring former All-Pro defensive lineman Dexter Lawrence and pairing him with free agents like Jonathan Allen and Boye Mafe should yield an improvement on that side of the ball. Plus, they got a really good young prospect in Cashius Howell with the 41st overall pick in the 2026 NFL Draft, which was a steal considering he could've gone in the first round. Conversely, losing star edge rusher Trey Hendrickson to their divisional rival has to sting. They also can't be thrilled with linebacker Logan Wilson retiring or Joseph Ossai and Geno Stone leaving in free agency. At the end of the day, though, the Bengals have a better defense on paper than they did before the start of last season, which is a good reason to feel hopeful for this year.

Cleveland Browns: Failure

If Myles Garrett isn't the best player in franchise history, then he is easily in the top five. Yet, the Browns decided to part ways with him in a trade for edge rusher Jared Verse and three draft picks. I don't hate the deal. Actually, I understand why the Browns made the trade when they did. Unfortunately, when you are often viewed as one of the worst-run organizations in North American sports, and you decide to part ways with your best player just a few months after he breaks the single-season NFL sack record, you're going to ruffle some feathers. The Browns had a good showing during the 2026 NFL Draft, but there are two major issues heading into this season: their coaching hire was uninspired, and they have one of the worst quarterback situations in the NFL. Though the future may seem bright, I can't in good conscience say that the 2026 off-season went well for the Browns.

Dallas Cowboys: Success

Failing to sign George Pickens to a long-term contract extension is a managerial failure that will greatly impact how the Cowboys handle the 2027 off-season. Beyond that, though, things are looking up in Dallas. I loved how they approached the 2026 NFL Draft. In fact, they had one of the three highest draft grades of all NFC teams. Safety Caleb Downs will be an immediate difference-maker on the defensive side of the ball. They also hired one of the most promising young defensive coordinators in the NFL by landing Christian Parker. They even managed to trade for a former Pro Bowl pass rusher in Rashan Gary. The defense should be much better this season, and the offense will still be one of the league's best, which means the 'Boys should have high hopes this year.

Denver Broncos: Success

The Broncos really didn't have to do much to have a fine off-season. They had a solid showing during the 2026 NFL Draft, even though Denver didn't make its first pick until the third round, where they found a replacement for Jonathan Franklin-Myers. They also made some shake-ups with the coaching staff by getting rid of offensive coordinator Joe Lombardi for Davis Webb. Denver re-signed several key contributors, including running back J.K. Dobbins, tight end Adam Trautman, linebacker Alex Singleton, and linebacker Justin Strnad. Their biggest acquisition came when they traded for wide receiver Jaylen Waddle, which cost them a first-rounder, a third-rounder, and a fourth-rounder, but they got a fourth-round pick in return. It was a successful off-season for the Broncos because they did not take a step back and should once again be amongst the AFC's best teams.

Detroit Lions: Failure

The Lions will be better than they were last season, but that doesn't mean that Detroit impressed during the Spring and Summer of 2026. With the unexpected retirement of Frank Ragnow and the release of Graham Glasgow, there will be a lot of pressure on center Cade Mays to ensure the offensive line improves. That's even more true for offensive tackle Blake Miller, who has big shoes to fill with former Pro Bowler Taylor Decker out of the picture. Detroit also had to part ways with running back David Montgomery, who was the yin to Jahymir Gibbs' yang. Lastly, I'm not sold on new offensive coordinator Drew Petzing, who was just fired from the same position in Arizona. If he wasn't good in Arizona, where there's no pressure, why will he be any better in Detroit, where the expectation is to win a Super Bowl? Even after they got an extension done with running back Jahmiyr Gibbs, this was not a stellar off-season for the Lions.

Green Bay Packers: Failure

There were plenty of positives that came out of this off-season for the Cheeseheads, like extending wide receivers Christian Watson and Jayden Reed. There were also some decisions Green Bay made that left us scratching our heads. Signing center Sean Rhyan and defensive tackle Devonte Wyatt are two major boom-or-bust moves that will greatly impact how this team performs during the regular season. It'll also be very interesting to see if new defensive coordinator Jonathan Gannon will be able to replicate the success he had with the Philadelphia Eagles. Meanwhile, Green Bay failed to add another veteran pass rusher and cornerback to help shore up the defense. This season will rely heavily on players remaining healthy, which is a major gamble to take in the NFL. This is a close call, but the Packers failed this off-season by a slim margin. As a Packers fan, I would’ve liked to see them operate with a little more urgency to win right now.

Houston Texans: Success

My good friend - and the most die-hard Texans fan I know - Scott Lindsay will tell you that it was a good off-season for Houston. Then again, he believes the Texans can do no wrong. As an unbiased observer, I can confirm that it WAS, in fact, a good off-season in H-Town (congrats Scotty). They gave extensions to some of their best players, like wide receiver Nico Collins, linebacker Azeez Al-Shaair, defensive end Danielle Hunter, and star pass rusher Will Anderson Jr. Getting that deal done this off-season will probably save Houston millions of dollars had they waited until next off-season to sign him to an extension. Additionally, I was impressed with the free agent additions and draft picks they made. Running back David Montgomery, offensive tackle Braden Smith, safety Reed Blankenship, rookie guard Keylan Rutledge, and rookie defensive lineman Kayden McDonald will have the Texans once again in the thick of the AFC Playoffs.

Indianapolis Colts: Success

There was no shortage of major moves in Indianapolis this off-season. They re-signed quarterback Daniel Jones to a two-year deal as well as wide receiver Alec Pierce and running back Jonathan Taylor. Not to mention, Indy added some players on the defensive side of the ball who should have a major impact this season. CJ Allen was the second-best linebacker prospect in the 2026 Draft Class, and he should be able to mitigate the loss of Zaire Franklin. Moreover, the Colts signed Arden Key for a reasonable $10 million per year. The 2025-26 season ended horribly because of Daniel Jones' injury. Before he hurt his leg, the Colts looked like one of the best teams in the AFC. Now that he has seemingly made a full recovery and Alec Pierce received a contract extension, nothing will distract Indy from snapping its five-year playoff drought.

Jacksonville Jaguars: Failure

Between the questionable draft strategy and the players this group lost in free agency, a lot of the positive sentiment this team built up during the 2025-26 regular season has since dissipated. The Jaguars had one of the worst showings of any franchise in the 2026 NFL Draft. They reached on several picks and failed to find immediate replacements for linebacker Devin Lloyd and safety Andrew Wingard. They signed tight end Brenton Strange to a $48 million extension just a few months after using their first draft pick on a tight end. The Jaguars also let Travis Etienne Jr. walk in free agency while signing two new running backs to make up for his lost production, but neither one of those guys provides the same offensive spark as Etienne. There will still be high expectations in Jacksonville despite the Jaguars’ unimpressive off-season.

Kansas City Chiefs: Success

There was a lot of turnover in Kansas City during the Spring and Summer. They lost a lot of important pieces like starting cornerbacks Jaylen Watson and Trent McDuffie, linebacker Leo Chanel, and offensive coordinator Matt Nagy. At the same time, they made some big additions through free agency and the draft. They signed running back Kenneth Walker III, who should resolve this team's longstanding issues with the rushing attack. Plus, KC hit three home runs with its first four draft picks by landing two starting-caliber cornerbacks, a promising defensive tackle, and a skilled edge rusher. The Chiefs also reunited with former offensive coordinator Eric Bieniemy. Best of all, quarterback Patrick Mahomes has been a full participant in practice less than a year after tearing his ACL and LCL, which means he should start every game this season. Kansas City will once again be a Super Bowl contender.

Las Vegas Raiders: Success

After being one of the worst teams in the NFL last season, the bar for a successful off-season was set fairly low. Still, that did not stop the Raiders from completely retooling their roster and coaching staff. If they had just drafted quarterback Fernando Mendoza and hired Klint Kubiak as the team's head coach, it would have sufficed. But Vegas went above and beyond this off-season. They spent millions of dollars improving the roster by signing Pro Bowl center Tyler Linderbaum, linebacker Quay Walker, running back Connor Heyward, quarterback Kirk Cousins, and linebacker Nakobe Dean. They also might have gotten the biggest steal in the NFL Draft when they used a fourth-rounder to take projected first-round pick Jermod McCoy. Granted, the Raiders play in a tough division and will be starting a rookie quarterback at some point this season, but that shouldn't take away from the fact that things are looking up in Las Vegas.

Los Angeles Chargers: Success

The 2025-26 season would've been very different for the Bolts had they not dealt with so many injuries to the offensive line. Thankfully, Los Angeles spared no expense in the trenches on both sides of the ball. Offensively, they signed center Tyler Biadasz and guard Cole Strange while re-signing Trevor Penning. On top of that, the Chargers used four draft picks on offensive linemen, including a second-rounder. If the Bolts once again deal with O-line injuries, they will be much better prepared this time around. Also, they added defensive lineman Dalvin Tomlinson, re-signed edge rusher Khalil Mack, and drafted Akheem Mesidor, who should be an immediate difference-maker getting after the quarterback. They might have lost Jesse Minter, but bringing in a brilliant offensive mind like Mike McDaniel will lead to even more success in Los Angeles. In reality, though, we won’t know if this was a good off-season or not until we see how the Chargers perform in the playoffs.

Los Angeles Rams: Success

What a rollercoaster ride of an off-season it has been for the Los Angeles Rams. First, they made a big splash in free agency by trading for cornerback Trent McDuffie and signing defensive back Jaylen Watson to a three-year deal. Then, they left everybody scratching their heads after the NFL Draft when they reached on quarterback Ty Simpson before using their second-round pick on a tight end, which is a position they’re already stacked at. Then, they made one of the biggest trades of the last few years by sending Jared Verse and three draft picks to Cleveland for reigning Defensive Player of the Year Myles Garrett. Plus, they signed Matthew Stafford to a one-year deal to keep him on the squad for at least another season. They had the best off-season of any team in the National Football League and look like the favorites to win the Super Bowl.

Miami Dolphins: Success

Fans are not going to be sold on the Miami Dolphins this season, but I believe they’re going to surprise some people after a top-to-bottom revamp of the franchise. They won’t be a playoff team, but the ‘Phins also won’t be as bad as everybody expects. They hired a new general manager and head coach, which allowed them to be aligned on the quarterback situation, so they signed Malik Willis to a three-year deal with an average annual value of $22.5 million. If he can be an average or slightly above average QB, then that looks like a good contract. Miami also extended their best player, running back De'Von Achane, which will help Willis' development. On top of all that, the Dolphins selected 13 players in the 2026 NFL Draft, and I wouldn't be surprised if at least four of those guys wind up becoming key contributors. Lastly, the Dolphins got rid of Tua Tagovailoa, which forced them to absorb a ton of dead money. However, making the move now gives Miami much more financial flexibility in the future.

Minnesota Vikings: Failure

Between a meh draft class and an unimpressive showing in free agency, the Vikes underwhelmed this off-season. The Kyler Murray signing was a fantastic move, but Minnesota might have been better served giving him a two-year deal. If he becomes the starter and plays well, then Murray will likely want to return to Minnesota for much more money, which they would have to give him next off-season. At the same time, if he failed to meet expectations, then a two-year deal would've given the Vikings a reliable backup for a little while. They also took a huge risk by drafting Caleb Banks with their first-round pick since he has a history of foot injuries. It would've been beneficial for this team to prioritize the secondary this off-season with the loss of Harrison Smith. Their weakness on the back-end could be disastrous in a division with three good QBs.

New England Patriots: Success

The Pats addressed every single need they had this off-season. The Super Bowl exposed issues along the offensive line, so they used their first-round pick on a versatile tackle after signing former Pro Bowl guard Alijah Vera-Tucker to a three-year deal in free agency. New England needed to find a new number-one wide receiver to replace Stefon Diggs. They got that and more by trading for A.J. Brown and signing Romeo Doubs. The Patriots also wanted to beef up the pass rush, so they signed a top-flight edge rusher in Dre'Mont Jones and used a second-round pick on Illinois' Gabe Jacas. Even getting Kevin Byard on a one-year deal will bring more veteran leadership to the defensive back room. After exceeding expectations last year, the New England Patriots did everything in their power to make sure they are just as competitive this season.

New Orleans Saints: Success

As the Saints continue to mold their roster to fit head coach Kellen Moore's scheme, they made many acquisitions to bolster the offense, which will aid the development of second-year quarterback Tyler Shough. New Orleans signed another high-level running back and restructured the deal of Alvin Kamara, which allowed them to give wide receiver Chris Olave a four-year extension. Plus, the Saints selected arguably the best wide receiver prospect in the 2026 NFL Draft when they took Jordyn Tyson with the eighth overall pick. They lost a few key contributors on the defensive side of the ball, but New Orleans' biggest concern should be the continued progression of Tyler Shough. If he takes another step forward this year, which I believe he will, then that will help fans get over some of the defensive issues.

New York Giants: Success

The Giants had arguably the best off-season of any team in the National Football League. They crushed the NFL Draft, nailed their head coaching hire, and added a lot of guys who will make a difference on this team through free agency. John Harbaugh was the best coach available this off-season, and he chose to go to New York. If that had been the only thing the front office changed about the team, then it would already be a successful off-season. However, Big Blue also signed multiple starters in free agency, including defensive linemen D.J. Reader and Shelby Harris, linebacker Tremaine Edmunds, cornerback Greg Newsome II, and tight end Isaiah Likely. Not to mention, New York crushed its draft by landing three future starters with their first three picks. The G-Men should have the biggest turnaround of any team in the league this season.

New York Jets: Success

The on-field product probably won't be amazing, but that doesn't mean the Jets had a bad off-season. Geno Smith will undoubtedly be a better quarterback than Justin Fields. They signed Breece Hall for under $46 million, which is pretty good considering the contracts given to Bijan Robinson, Jonathan Taylor, and Jahmiyr Gibbs. The defense should produce more turnovers, too, with cornerback Nahshon Wright, linebacker Demario Davis, defensive tackle T'Vondre Sweat, and safety Minkah Fitzpatrick now in the mix. Gang Green also had a stellar draft, landing AT LEAST four guys who should start and make an immediate impact on the team this season. The biggest acquisition is number-two overall pick David Bailey, who will completely change the Jets' defense and be a franchise cornerstone moving forward.

Philadelphia Eagles: Success

I can't remember the last time the Eagles had a bad off-season. Wide receiver A.J. Brown had become a major distraction in the locker room, so they managed to offload him and his gaudy contract while getting a future first-round draft pick in return. Off-season acquisitions Dontayvion Wicks, Marquise Brown, Eli Stowers, and Makai Lemon should be able to replicate Brown's production and then some this year. Plus, Philly added some defensive difference-makers, including two former Pro Bowlers in cornerback Tariq Woolen and edge rusher Jonathan Greenard. We can't forget the major contract extension they also gave to defensive tackle Jordan Davis, which gives them more stability in the trenches and avoids any potential holdout next off-season. Lastly, I have high hopes for new offensive coordinator Sean Mannion, who has been getting rave reviews throughout the Spring and Summer.

Pittsburgh Steelers: Failure

With the players that Pittsburgh added through free agency and the draft, one could argue that this was a good off-season for the Steelers. They shored up the O-line with Max Iheanachor and Gennings Dunker, while adding more offensive weapons like Michael Pittman and Rico Dowdle. The defense also has some new faces that will make a big impact this season, such as Jamin Davis, Jamel Dean, and Jaquan Brisker. Unfortunately, the Steelers are still going to be leaning on Aaron Rodgers to man the quarterback spot. And though he is a future Hall of Famer, it's risky to base your team’s success on a 42-year-old QB who has recently brought controversy everywhere he has gone. Also, I was underwhelmed by the Mike McCarthy hire. It does not feel like he is an improvement over former coach Mike Tomlin. That reason alone makes this a bad off-season.

San Francisco 49ers: Failure

It's difficult to put a label on the Niners' off-season. It wasn't a roaring success, but it also wasn't an obvious failure. They managed to bring back star offensive lineman Trent Williams, but they're going to be paying him $37 million guaranteed for only two seasons of service. They found an incredible defensive coordinator, but that's only because they lost Robert Saleh to the Tennessee Titans. San Fran added some offensive weapons through the NFL Draft, but they reached on a few picks, including third-round running back Kaelon Black. As I explained in April, it's never a good sign when your general manager and head coach have to defend their draft strategy to the media. Lastly, head coach Kyle Shanahan was in a messy car accident and is still feeling its residual effects. The biggest issue is that the Niners are dealing with injuries to almost 20 players, even though we’re still a month away from the start of the regular season. We can't really call this a successful off-season for San Francisco when it has been overshadowed by so many negatives.

Seattle Seahawks: Success

This team won a Super Bowl just six months ago. Therefore, fans will be satisfied with any move the team makes, which is why we can call this off-season a success. They might have lost Super Bowl MVP Kenneth Walker III, safety Coby Bryant, and edge rusher Boye Mafe, but they also found highly-qualified replacements and re-signed the best players on the team. They landed the second-best running back in the 2026 NFL Draft as well as a highly-touted safety and cornerback. Not to mention, they re-signed cornerback Josh Jobe and reigning Offensive Player of the Year Jaxon Smith-Njigba. Plus, Seattle found a strong candidate in Brian Fleury to replace offensive coordinator Klint Kubiak. After teams win Super Bowls, their rosters and coaching staffs are usually ravaged by other organizations. All things considered, though, the Seahawks weathered that storm and should once again be one of the best teams in the NFL.

Tampa Bay Buccaneers: Failure

The Buccaneers had three tasks that they needed to accomplish this off-season: get rid of head coach Todd Bowles, sign Baker Mayfield to a contract extension, and find a new pass-rusher. They only did one of those things, and the pass-rusher they got is a rookie who probably won't have as much of an impact as a seasoned veteran. Keeping Todd Bowles is a highly questionable decision after last season ended so poorly, but it is somewhat justifiable since the guy has had success with the Bucs. However, it’s unacceptable that Tampa failed to get a new deal done with Baker Mayfield. In fact, it sounds like the Buccaneers botched the negotiations so badly that Mayfield is now "a little pissed." To top it all off, Vita Vea, the team's best defender, formally requested a trade last month, which means the Bucs might lose their best offensive AND defensive players next off-season. Even if they keep both guys, they will have to pay them much more than originally anticipated. This wasn't a failed off-season for Tampa Bay; it was a full-fledged disaster.

Tennessee Titans: Success

Outside of the New York Giants, the Titans did the most to ensure their team does a full 180 this coming season by nailing their coaching hires, free agency moves, and draft picks. Tennessee's new coach, offensive coordinator, and defensive coordinator have all had head coaching experience elsewhere in the NFL, which should lead to improvements in all three phases of the game. From a free agency perspective, this was a franchise-changing off-season. They signed at least four guys who will step in right away and become key starters on offense and defense (wide receiver Wan'Dale Robinson, defensive lineman John Franklin-Myers, cornerback Cordale Flott, and cornerback Alonta Taylor). They also traded for two stud defensive linemen in Jermaine Johnson II and Solomon Thomas, while signing defensive tackle Jeffrey Simmons to a three-year extension. Lastly, Tennessee found a number-one wide receiver in the draft as well as a good edge rusher, linebacker, and center. The Titans had an excellent off-season.

Washington Commanders: Success

Some people will be disappointed with the decision to bring back Dan Quinn, but I’m not one of those individuals. This guy has been to a Super Bowl before as a head coach and is just two years removed from an NFC Championship Game appearance with a rookie QB. The defense should be MUCH better after the Commanders signed several guys who should get playing time this season, including edge rusher K'Lavon Chaisson, defensive lineman Odafe Oweh, nose tackle Tim Settle, linebacker Leo Chanel, safety Nick Cross, and cornerback Amik Robertson. Fans should also be very excited about rookie linebacker Sonny Styles, who will completely change this defense’s speed. The offense will look good as well with a new tight end and more stability along the offensive line. They will undoubtedly be hurt by the loss of tackle Laremy Tunsil, but the front office did a nice job setting up the Commanders to be more competitive this year.
