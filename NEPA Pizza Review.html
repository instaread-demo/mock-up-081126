Primo’s Pizza and Pasta in East Stroudsburg: Solid, Foldable, Quintessential Pocono Pizza

I walked in, ordered, paid the bill, and reviewed the pizza like every other customer. No heads-up. No comps.

Every time I head into the Poconos for pizza, I ask myself the same question walking through the door: “Will this be the memorable slice that stands out from the rest?” It’s not a cynical question — it’s an honest one, born of a lot of Pocono pizza miles. Because here’s the truth I’ve come to accept about pizza in this part of NEPA:

The Poconos are absolutely loaded with good, solid, expertly crafted New York style pizza. Thin, foldable, crispy, quality ingredients, honest execution — you can walk into just about any long-running pizza shop between Stroudsburg and Mount Pocono and get a really enjoyable slice. It’s a genuinely delicious pizza market. But the shop that truly stands out from the pack, the one that makes you drive back across two counties for another visit — that’s a harder find.

That’s the lens I brought with me to Primo’s Pizza and Pasta at 156 Eagles Glen Mall #102 in East Stroudsburg — a locally-owned Pocono pizza staple that’s been serving the community since 1995. Primo’s had been recommended to me a good number of times as one of the better pizzas in East Stroudsburg and the greater Poconos, so it was time to see for myself.

A Familiar Slice from an Unfamiliar Kitchen

Here’s a fun wrinkle. I stopped by planning to try a couple slices for the first time — but before I walked in, I did what I always do: I checked my pizza review database. And to my surprise, I realized I had actually tried Primo’s pizza once before.

It was at a work luncheon where a coworker brought in a Primo’s Sicilian pizza. I remember it clearly: baked to a deep, well-done finish on a pillowy yet chewy Sicilian crust, with a mellow sauce and a generous layer of cheese. It was a good tray. I logged the notes at the time, sat with the scorecard, and moved on — not realizing I’d cross paths with Primo’s again as a walk-in customer years later.

So this visit officially becomes my second Primo’s experience — this time for their New York style slice program.

Atmosphere: A Locally-Owned Pocono Staple

Primo’s sits in the Eagles Glen shopping center on Business Route 209 in East Stroudsburg, and it wears its longevity comfortably. This is a family-owned pizzeria and Italian restaurant that’s been feeding the Poconos since 1995 — decades of showing up and cranking out slices, trays, hoagies, and pasta plates for the community. That kind of tenure only happens when the food is legitimately good and the neighborhood keeps coming back.

The menu is the classic Pocono Italian-American full spread: round pies, Sicilian, gourmet pies (Margherita, Hawaiian, White, BBQ Chicken, Spinach, Broccoli, Vegetarian), stuffed pies, hoagies, cheesesteaks, salads, pasta entrées, veal, chicken, and seafood. It’s a real “there’s something for everyone” operation, and it’s clearly built for feeding families.

The slice case up front is where the action happens if you’re stopping in during the day for a quick lunch, and that’s exactly what I did.

The Slice Case Visit

I was in a time crunch, so a fresh cheese pie coming out of the oven wasn’t in the cards — that’s on the return-visit list. Instead, I ordered from the slice case:

  • A New York Style Margherita Pizza slice
  • A New York Style Buffalo Chicken (Hot Wing) Pizza slice

Two different personalities, one visit. Here’s how each one showed up.

The Margherita Slice

Visually, this was a beautiful slice. Built on a thinly stretched crust that came across as chewy with a slight crisp. Generously loaded — enough sauce and cheese that the slice had a significant flop under the weight when I picked it up. That’s a real Pocono New York slice signature and I don’t mind it at all; it’s part of the fold-and-eat experience.

Flavor breakdown:

  • The sauce was modest in overall flavor with a unique sweetness and tang. Not the punchiest tomato I’ve had in NEPA, and I didn’t detect much in the way of herbal seasoning coming through. The primary flavor note I picked up was actually the nutty character of the parmesan cheese doing a lot of the aromatic work, with some light garlic in the background.
  • The mozzarella was quality and generously applied. A solid melt, good stretch, plenty of cheese coverage.

Solid, likable, honest New York style slice from the case. The main note I’d flag on the margherita is that I’d love to see the sauce push a little harder — a punchier tomato with a bit more seasoning would elevate the whole slice significantly, because the crust and cheese are already right where they need to be.

The Buffalo Chicken (Hot Wing) Slice

Now this one I really enjoyed.

The hot wing slice came loaded with a ton of diced fried chicken, plenty of cheese, and a buffalo sauce that leaned to the sweeter side with just a hint of heat. Looking at the slice in the case, I actually made a preemptive call and ordered an extra side of mild buffalo sauce on the assumption that the slice might come out a little dry.

I was wrong.

Here’s what I discovered on the first bite — Primo’s applies the buffalo sauce under the cheese, not on top. That’s a legitimate pro move in my book, and I want to give the pizzaiolo credit for it. Applying sauce under the cheese does two important things: it protects the sauce from breaking down and burning in the oven, and it keeps the moisture locked into the chicken so the topping stays juicy. I didn’t need the extra sauce at all — there was plenty under the hood.

The crust on this one was also foldable and bent under the weight of the hefty toppings, but it was tasty, and the whole thing came together as a really enjoyable slice.

The Quintessential Pocono Pizza

Zooming out on both slices together, I want to say this clearly and honestly: Primo’s is serving quintessential Pocono pizza. Good, solid, foldable, well-crafted New York style slices made with quality ingredients. Both slices were enjoyable. No complaints from the pizza guy.

Combined with the previous Sicilian pizza experience from the work luncheon — a deep-baked, well-done Sicilian on a pillowy-yet-chewy crust with mellow sauce and generous cheese — Primo’s is delivering a consistent, quality product across pizza styles that clearly resonates with their long-standing customer base.

Is this the memorable slice that stands out from the rest of the Poconos? Honestly, no — and I’m not sure any single Pocono shop I’ve reviewed to date owns that crown yet. But is this exactly on par with the excellent, dependable New York style slice you find throughout the region? Absolutely yes. It’s exactly what Pocono pizza is at its best, and there’s real value in a shop that’s been delivering that quality consistently for 30 years.

Pizzaiolo’s Notes 🍕

A few notes from the pizzaiolo seat looking at the full Primo’s picture:

  • The buffalo sauce under the cheese is a legitimate technique win. A lot of hot wing pizzas in the region put the buffalo sauce on top where it discolors, burns, and breaks down in the oven. Primo’s placing it under the cheese is a small but professional detail that shows attention to craft.
  • The parmesan-forward flavor profile on the margherita is unusual and worth calling out. Most NY style slices lean on tomato and mozzarella as the two lead voices; here, the parmesan is stepping into the spotlight because the tomato is playing quiet. Not wrong, just distinctive.
  • The Sicilian tray from the work luncheon scored the highest of the three pies I’ve tried at Primo’s — 7.6 overall. That deep, well-done bake with a pillowy chewy crust is where I think the kitchen really shines. Sicilian lovers should absolutely try this.
  • Two easy improvements would elevate the slice program further: a punchier tomato with more seasoning, and a slightly longer bake to crisp up the crust a bit more. Both would push the New York slices closer to the top of the Pocono pack.
  • 30 years in business is real credibility. Shops don’t survive three decades in the Poconos serving mediocre food. Primo’s is doing something right at a fundamental level.

What I’d Order Next Time

The obvious return trip is to catch a fresh cheese pie coming out of the oven — that’s the honest baseline test for any New York style shop and I couldn’t wait for one on this visit. I also want to try the Sicilian again as a formal, on-site review rather than a work-luncheon tray. And their gourmet pizza list — Margherita as a full pie, Spinach, Broccoli, BBQ Chicken, or the Sundried Tomato Pizza DiscoverNEPA mentions — has some interesting options worth exploring.

Final Thoughts: Solid, Dependable, Pocono to the Core

Primo’s Pizza and Pasta earns exactly what its 30-year tenure suggests: a spot in the reliable, well-crafted, quintessential Pocono pizza tier. The slices I tried were both enjoyable, honestly executed, and served up in the classic Pocono foldable New York style that this region genuinely does well across many shops. The hot wing slice was a highlight thanks to the sauce-under-the-cheese technique and the generous chicken load. The margherita is a good slice with room to grow on the sauce side. The Sicilian — from a prior encounter — was arguably the strongest of the three pies and deserves another formal look.

If you’re in East Stroudsburg or passing through the Poconos and looking for a legit, family-owned pizzeria with three decades of neighborhood credibility, Primo’s is a very safe, very solid pick.

My Takeaways

  • 🍕 Solid, foldable, well-crafted quintessential Pocono New York style pizza
  • 🍅 Sauce is modest in flavor — a punchier, more seasoned tomato would elevate the slice program
  • 🧀 Quality mozzarella, generously applied — parmesan is doing more of the aromatic work than usual
  • 🍕 Hot wing slice is the winner of the visit — sauce under the cheese is a pro move
  • 🍕 The Sicilian from a prior tasting is the highest-scoring pie so far — worth a formal return visit
  • 🍕 30 years family-owned in the Eagles Glen shopping center — a Pocono staple with earned credibility
